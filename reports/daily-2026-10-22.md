### Jobs and Skills Australia

**Occupation shortages continue to ease, while new analysis reveals what's driving persistent gaps**
*Published: 2026-10-21 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

The 2026 Occupation Shortage List (OSL) shows 279 occupations in national shortage (27%), down from 29% in 2025 and a peak of 36% in 2023. Despite the overall easing, shortages remain concentrated in Technicians and Trades Workers and Professionals — construction, engineering, electrotechnology, telecommunications, health, and early childhood education. A new Occupation Shortage Drivers (OSD) analysis identifies training pipeline constraints, lack of suitable applicants, and retention issues as the three key drivers; training is the main driver for occupations like Motor Mechanics. For RTOs, this confirms continued demand for qualifications in construction, electrical, and health trades, and provides evidence to support student enrolment messages into shortage areas.

**Job ads hold steady nationally in September 2026**
*Published: 2026-10-21 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

September 2026 IVI data shows national job ad volumes holding steady overall, but the strongest decreases were for Technicians and Trades Workers and Sales Workers. Read alongside the 2026 OSL (above), the pattern is consistent: fewer employers actively advertising trades roles while vacancy rates and shortages persist, suggesting hiring friction rather than a demand drop. RTOs should note that softening job ads for trades is not a signal to reduce delivery in shortage occupations.
