## Top items this week

### 1. AVETMISS is being replaced — VET Information Standard now law

**Source:** ASQA Latest News | **Published:** 2026-08-12 | **Relevance:** HIGH

DEWR and NCVER have gazetted the National Vocational Education and Training Regulator (Data Provision Requirements) Amendment (Data Streamlining) Instrument 2026, effective 1 October 2026. The instrument replaces the AVETMISS VET Provider Collection Specifications with a new VET Information Standard, changing how RTOs collect and submit student activity data.

Key transition timelines:
- RTOs registered before 1 Jan 2028 (most existing providers) can continue AVETMISS reporting until they choose to transition, but **must complete transition by 1 January 2029**.
- From **1 October 2026**, new data system requirements apply: RTOs must store reportable data digitally and support reporting through the required pathway.

This is the most significant change to RTO data reporting obligations in years. Student management system vendors will need to update their products; RTOs should confirm their SMS is on a compliant roadmap.

URL: https://www.asqa.gov.au/node/720

### 2. ASIC disqualified 36 directors in 2025-26 — a sharp rise

**Source:** ASIC Newsroom | **Published:** 2026-08-10 | **Relevance:** MEDIUM

ASIC's annual enforcement summary for 2025-26 reported 150 administrative enforcement outcomes, including 36 director disqualifications from managing corporations — described as a sharp year-on-year increase. The summary covers the full financial sector, but the director disqualification figures are directly relevant to RTO owners, who carry Corporations Act obligations as company directors in addition to their Standards compliance obligations.

The rise in disqualifications reflects ASIC's continued focus on director accountability, including in smaller companies. RTO owners who are also company directors should be aware that Corporations Act obligations run parallel to ASQA obligations — a Standards breach won't insulate a director from ASIC action.

URL: https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-185mr-asic-protects-consumers-by-removing-high-risk-financial-sector-participants/

### 3. New JSA occupation profiles tool useful for RTO marketing and counselling

**Source:** Jobs and Skills Australia | **Published:** 2026-08-07 | **Relevance:** MEDIUM

Jobs and Skills Australia launched OSCA (Occupation Skills and Career Atlas) occupation profiles — a free tool pulling together labour market data and detailed occupation information in a single interface. RTOs can use it to benchmark demand for the occupations their courses lead into, support evidence-based claims in marketing materials, and inform career counselling conversations with prospective students. Useful for any RTO building or reviewing course offerings against current market conditions.

URL: https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/news/explore-australias-occupations-new-osca-occupation-profiles

### 4. GBV Regulator publishes National Code confidentiality guidance

**Source:** ITECA News | **Published:** 2026-08-13 | **Relevance:** MEDIUM

The Gender-Based Violence Regulator published a Statement of Regulatory Expectations covering confidentiality, non-disclosure agreements, and compliance with the National Code of Practice for Providers of Education and Training to Overseas Students. Primarily aimed at registered higher education providers, but CRICOS-registered RTOs that must comply with the National Code are also in scope. Clarifies how providers should handle confidentiality arrangements in GBV matters under the Code.

URL: https://www.iteca.edu.au/web/news/Articles/The%20Gender-Based%20Violence%20(GBV)%20Regulator%20publishes%20confidentiality%20guidance.aspx
