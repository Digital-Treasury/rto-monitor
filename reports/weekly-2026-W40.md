## Lead items

**1. CPC Release 11.0 training package error — four qualifications incorrectly deleted**
*Source: training.gov.au | Published: 2026-09-25 | Relevance: HIGH*

Four CPC (Construction, Plumbing and Construction) training products — CPC31220, CPC40120, CPCCPB3014, CPCCPB3027 — were incorrectly deleted from training.gov.au when Release 11.0 was published on 23 September 2026. The error was identified and corrected by 25 September; the products are now current. RTOs delivering these qualifications should confirm their scope of registration is correct and check that enrolments were not disrupted during the deletion window.

URL: https://training.gov.au

**3. NCVER: VET completion rates at 48.9% — national benchmark released**
*Source: NCVER News | Published: 2026-09-29 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

NCVER's VET Qualification Completion Rates 2025 report finds 48.9% of qualifications commenced in 2021 were completed within four years, down slightly from 49.5% for the 2020 cohort. Projected rates for 2022 and 2023 are higher, suggesting improvement. NCVER emphasises that non-completion does not mean failure — many learners exit with partial skills that lead directly to employment. Key benchmark for RTOs assessing their own performance.

URL: https://www.ncver.edu.au/news-and-events/media-releases/vet-completion-rates-highlight-diversity-of-learner-pathways-and-outcomes

**4. IT job market has shifted — demand weakness, not skills shortage**
*Source: Jobs and Skills Australia | Published: 2026-09-24 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

JSA's REOS Spotlight for IT reveals the sector has moved from recruitment difficulty to a lack of demand for products and services. Only 16% of IT employers expect to increase staffing next quarter — lowest in recent years. The recruitment rate (32%) sits well below the all-industry average (49%). RTOs delivering IT qualifications should review how they're marketing outcomes: the skills-shortage narrative no longer fits the current data.

URL: https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/news/changing-recruitment-trends-it-industry

**5. ASIC enforcement: two director actions this week**
*Source: ASIC Newsroom | Published: 2026-09-25, 2026-09-29 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

Two ASIC enforcement actions this week underscore personal liability for company directors. Former Queensland property developer Ian Omar Chester was sentenced to 9 years imprisonment for fraud through a managed investment scheme (29 Sep). Former mining company director Yuetu Ma was disqualified from managing corporations for four years (25 Sep). Neither involves an RTO, but RTO owners are company directors carrying identical legal obligations under the Corporations Act.
