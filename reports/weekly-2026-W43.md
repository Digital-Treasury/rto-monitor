## Lead items this week

### 1. TLI Training Package Release 17.0 — action required

TLI (Transport and Logistics) Release 17.0 was published on 16 October 2026. RTOs delivering any TLI qualifications, skill sets or units of competency must review their training and assessment materials against the new release to confirm currency. A brief technical issue on publication day (some products incorrectly shown as removed) was resolved; all products are restored to 'Current' status on training.gov.au.

Relevance: HIGH | Source: training.gov.au | Captured: Oct 19

### 2. Directors' duties: four ASIC actions in one week

A cluster of four ASIC enforcement actions this week reinforces the personal liability exposure for company directors:

- **Former Rex CEO and executive chair disqualified 4 years / $800,000 penalty** for breaching directors' duties and continuous disclosure obligations (Oct 20).
- **Visy/Pratt group fined $4.1M** in infringement notices for failing to lodge financial reports on time — 21 companies across the group (Oct 21).
- **ASIC's Annual Report** reveals $830M in court-ordered civil penalties for 2025–26, with director disqualifications a central enforcement theme (Oct 19).
- **ASIC seeks to appeal** a NSW Supreme Court ruling that Rex non-executive directors did not breach their duties by relying on management's profit forecast — the appeal could tighten what scrutiny is expected of board members (Oct 23).

RTO owners are company directors under the Corporations Act. The same rules apply.

Relevance: MEDIUM | Sources: ASIC Newsroom

### 3. 2026 Occupation Shortage List — training pipeline is the bottleneck

The 2026 OSL confirms shortages are concentrated in Technicians and Trades Workers and Professionals — construction, engineering, electrotechnology, telecommunications, health, and early childhood education. A new Occupation Shortage Drivers (OSD) analysis identifies training pipeline constraints as the leading cause of persistent shortages (e.g., Motor Mechanics). Shortages fell overall to 27% of assessed occupations, down from a peak of 36% in 2023, but the trades/health concentration is deepening.

For RTOs: this is policy-backed evidence that demand for qualifications in electrical, construction, and health trades remains high. Useful for enrolment marketing and funding conversations.

Relevance: MEDIUM | Source: Jobs and Skills Australia | Captured: Oct 22

### 4. JSA Jobs & Skills Report 2026 — AI reshaping skills demand

Jobs and Skills Australia released its inaugural "Pathways to Success – Jobs and Skills Report 2026" for national Jobs & Skills Month, focusing on how AI is reshaping Australia's workforce and skills needs. The report signals which qualifications and capabilities are likely to attract future government funding attention. A companion opinion piece explored building a connected skills language across education, training and work. RTOs delivering continuous professional development, digital, or future-of-work programs are well positioned in this framing.

Relevance: MEDIUM | Source: Jobs and Skills Australia | Captured: Oct 20

### 5. AI in VET admin and compliance — first structured sector program

ITECA's FSO Skills Accelerator AI initiative launched this week: an action-learning program where VET practitioners identify real compliance and admin challenges, co-design AI-assisted solutions, and test them. This is the first structured, sector-specific program for applying AI to RTO operations (compliance documentation, reporting, admin workflows). A discovery workshop is the entry point.

Relevance: MEDIUM | Source: ITECA News | Captured: Oct 21
