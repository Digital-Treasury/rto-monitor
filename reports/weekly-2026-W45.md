## Lead items — highest significance

### 1. ASIC director enforcement: three actions in one week (MEDIUM — recurring pattern)
*Multiple items: 2026-10-29 to 2026-11-04 | Source: ASIC Newsroom*

ASIC ran a concentrated burst of enforcement activity affecting company directors:

- **Financial reporting surveillance fall short** (Oct 29): ASIC flagged continuing failures in financial reporting and audit quality across the market, calling for improvement.
- **Youpla Group CEO and director breach** (Oct 30): Federal Court confirmed that the CEO and a director of an Aboriginal funeral insurance company breached their duties by failing to act in good faith and in company interests.
- **iSignthis appeal dismissed** (Nov 3): The Federal Court upheld $11 million in penalties and a director disqualification against iSignthis and former CEO on appeal — confirming enforcement outcomes are durable.
- **$4.3M in infringement notices to 22 companies** (Nov 4): ASIC issued infringement notices to large proprietary companies for failure to lodge financial reports on time.

RTO owners operating as company directors are subject to the same obligations. The pattern this week is ASIC making clear that financial reporting lapses, director duty breaches, and governance failures will attract enforcement regardless of sector.

Content angles: Director obligations checklist for year-end; ASIC's enforcement posture is not softening.

### 2. TEQSA antisemitism SRE — dual-sector deadline 1 January 2027 (MEDIUM)
*Published: 2026-10-29 | Source: TEQSA News*

TEQSA published its Statement of Regulatory Expectations (SRE) requiring higher education providers to adopt a definition of antisemitism and integrate it into complaints processes, staff and student guidance, and institutional decision-making by 1 January 2027. RTOs that also hold TEQSA registration (dual-sector providers) must act on this within two months. ASQA-only RTOs are not directly affected.

URL: https://www.teqsa.gov.au/about-us/news-and-events/latest-news/teqsa-publishes-sre-adopting-definition-antisemitism

### 3. National Skills Taxonomy — polyhierarchy explained (MEDIUM)
*Published: 2026-11-02 | Source: Jobs and Skills Australia*

JSA published the recording of the second NST webinar. JSA Deputy Commissioner Megan Lilly introduced the concept of a skills polyhierarchy: organising skills so they are discoverable through multiple pathways rather than confined to rigid categories. This directly shapes how future training packages are structured. RTOs involved in curriculum design or training package development should understand the direction the taxonomy is taking.

URL: https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/news/webinar-more-one-way-find-skill

### 4. ITECA: invest in students, not institutions (MEDIUM)
*Published: 2026-11-02 | Source: ITECA News*

ITECA argues that VET funding under the National Skills Agreement is structurally captured by institutional interests rather than flowing to student outcomes. The piece frames the problem as systemic: publicly funded RTOs can absorb subsidised places that independent providers cannot access, regardless of comparative quality or completion rates. Useful framing for independent RTOs in funding conversations with governments.

URL: https://www.iteca.edu.au/web/news/Articles/2026/National_Update_-_We%20need%20to%20invest%20in%20students,%20not%20institutions.aspx

RTOs delivering veterinary nursing qualifications should participate — feedback now directly shapes the final qualification structure and units.
