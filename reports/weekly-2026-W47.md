## Lead items (most significant)

### 1. ASIC flags AI and tech as 2027 enforcement priorities — relevant for RTO directors
*Source: ASIC Newsroom | Published: 18 November 2026 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

ASIC has released its 2027 enforcement priorities, with two headline areas: financial misconduct driving cost of living pressures, and AI or technology that causes consumer harm. For RTO owners as company directors, this matters on two levels. First, director duties under the Corporations Act apply regardless of industry — ASIC's broadening of AI scrutiny signals it will examine how businesses use AI in ways that could harm consumers or students. Second, RTOs integrating AI into marketing, student support, or outcomes claims now operate in a riskier compliance environment.

### 2. ASIC bans former director of 8 companies for 5 years
*Source: ASIC Newsroom | Published: 17 November 2026 | Relevance: MEDIUM*

ASIC banned Benjamin Godfrey, former director of ISG Financial Services and seven related companies, from managing corporations for five years following a string of company failures. The case reinforces that ASIC consistently pursues directors who fail their statutory duties — it does not limit enforcement to financial services. RTO owners who are company directors of their training organisations carry the same obligations under the Corporations Act: due diligence, care, and acting in the company's best interests.

Content-worthy: YES
LinkedIn angle: ASIC bans director of 8 failed companies for 5 years — a practical reminder for RTO owners: your director obligations under the Corporations Act are real, and ASIC enforces them regardless of industry.
